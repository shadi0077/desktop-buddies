Desktop Buddies

Two desktop-pet apps for your Mac, built on one engine. Characters who walk about, react to your cursor, do the things their source material had them do, and talk to each other when more than one is out.

🦜 Desktop Buddies

Nine Microsoft Agent characters, from the era when software came with a cartoon in the corner. They have voices, tell jokes, know facts, ask riddles, sing actual melodies in tune, and argue with each other — in English and in Najdi Arabic.

🎮 MegaDrive Buddies

Thirty-seven sixteen-bit sprites who pace the length of the screen at their own speeds and square up when two of them get close. They talk in speech bubbles with no voice, about games up to 1997 and nothing after, and grunt with the sound effects out of their own games.

🩹 SNES Buddies

Twenty Super Nintendo characters, from sixteen games acquired all at once — which is why almost nothing about cutting them generalised. They talk in speech bubbles like the Mega Drive cast, and they are entirely silent: no rip of these games' audio is to hand, and borrowing Sega's grunts for Nintendo's characters would be worse than nothing.

The idea

The Bonzi idea, minus the part everyone remembers it for: no network access, no analytics, no bundled anything, no browser changes, no upsell. They are windows that draw a character. Quit them from the menu bar and they're gone. Both apps carry their own bundle identifier, so you can run them at once and each keeps its own characters, positions, volume and size.

Alive, not looping

Energy that rises with attention and decays without it. They notice your cursor, get bored of being poked, and settle down when you leave the keyboard.

They talk to each other

Aloud in one app and in speech bubbles in the other — taking turns, facing each other, nobody starting a line while somebody else is mid-sentence.

Their own volume

A slider that's theirs alone, independent of the system volume. Mute them in one click.

Nothing phones home

No network code in either app. Everything they know is bundled, and always will be.

One codebase, three apps

A product is a JSON manifest naming a cast. The build copies only that cast's sprites — and only the frames its animations actually reference — then stamps the name and bundle id into the app. At runtime the app filters its roster and its menus to match, so an app where nobody has a voice has no Language submenu and offers Let Them Fight rather than Let Them Chat.

./build.sh                    # all three
./build.sh snes-buddies       # just that one

Adding an app is a JSON file: no target, no project file, and nothing in the Swift sources needs to know it exists. SNES Buddies was the third.

Getting them running

macOS 11 Big Sur or later, on Apple Silicon, plus the Xcode command line tools. A clone builds and runs directly — the sprites are included.

git clone https://github.com/shadi0077/desktop-buddies.git
cd desktop-buddies
./build.sh
open "build/Desktop Buddies.app" "build/MegaDrive Buddies.app" "build/SNES Buddies.app"
